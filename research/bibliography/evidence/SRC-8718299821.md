# Evidence — Safe Reinforcement Learning via Shielding

## E1 — Shielding separates formal safety enforcement from policy learning

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Section 1
- **Claim:** The shield is a separate runtime component that enforces a temporal-logic safety specification independently of how quickly or successfully the RL policy converges.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Alshiekh et al. synthesize a reactive shield from the safety specification and an abstraction of environment dynamics and insert it between the learning agent and environment. The shield interferes only when needed to prevent unsafe behavior, so correctness of executed behavior is not made contingent on convergence of the learned policy.

### Context and limits

The guarantee depends on the specification and the environment abstraction used to synthesize the shield. Safety of the executed system should therefore not be attributed solely to the learned policy.

### Thesis use

Record proposed actions, executed actions, and shield interventions separately.

### Citation

Alshiekh et al. (2018), Abstract and Section 1.

## E2 — Preemptive and post-posed shielding change the learner interface differently

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 1; Figures 1–2
- **Claim:** Preemptive shielding gives the learner a set of safe actions before action selection, whereas post-posed shielding monitors and corrects an action already selected by the learner.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

In the preemptive architecture, the shield restricts the action set available to the learner before the decision is made. In the post-posed architecture, the learner proposes an action normally and the shield replaces it only if executing that action would violate the safety specification.

### Context and limits

The two architectures provide different information and capability advantages. Action masking can simplify the learning problem in addition to enforcing safety.

### Thesis use

Report `shield_mode`, available-action reduction, override fraction, and any nominal-performance impact.

### Citation

Alshiekh et al. (2018), Section 1 and Figures 1–2.

## E3 — Shielding requires prior formal knowledge

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 1–2
- **Claim:** The shield is computed from a safety specification and an abstraction of relevant environment dynamics, so its guarantee relies on information unavailable to a purely model-free learner.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The framework begins from explicit temporal-logic requirements and an abstract model sufficient to reason about safety. The synthesis process is intended to use only the parts of the environment relevant to enforcing the specification, but this still constitutes prior structural knowledge.

### Thesis use

Include specification/model access in the fairness ledger and do not compare shielded and unshielded agents as if they received identical prior information.

### Citation

Alshiekh et al. (2018), Sections 1–2.

## E4 — Shield interventions are not learned recovery

- **Type:** scope inference grounded in the architecture
- **Location:** Sections 1–6
- **Claim:** Shielding prevents unsafe actions at runtime; it does not identify environmental changepoints or constitute a learned post-shift recovery mechanism.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The shield filters or corrects actions according to a precomputed correctness condition. An intervention can keep the executed trajectory safe while the underlying learned policy remains unchanged or poorly adapted.

### Thesis use

Keep safety-intervention rate separate from change-detection and recovery metrics.

### Citation

Alshiekh et al. (2018), overall architecture and evaluation.
