# Evidence — Robust Control of Markov Decision Processes with Uncertain Transition Matrices

## E1 — Nominal optimal policies can be sensitive to transition-probability estimation error
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction
- **Claim:** Errors in estimated transition matrices can materially change the behavior and performance of an MDP policy, motivating explicit robust control when the transition model is uncertain.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Nilim and El Ghaoui motivate robust MDP control by observing that transition probabilities often have to be estimated from limited data and that nominal MDP solutions can be highly sensitive to those estimates. Their robust formulation therefore optimizes against a set of plausible transition matrices rather than treating one estimated matrix as exact.

### Thesis use
Use as foundational support for a static robust baseline under transition-model uncertainty.

### Citation
Nilim and El Ghaoui (2005), Abstract and Introduction.

## E2 — Rectangular uncertainty enables robust dynamic programming
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 2.2; Section 3.1
- **Claim:** Under the paper's rectangular uncertainty property, the time-varying robust-control game admits a Bellman-style robust dynamic-programming recursion.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The uncertainty set is factorized across rows of the transition matrices, so nature can choose a next-state distribution from the local admissible set associated with the current state and action. The resulting robust Bellman recursion replaces the nominal expected continuation value with the worst continuation value over that local set.

### Context and limits
Rectangularity excludes correlations among uncertain transition rows/actions and is a structural tractability assumption.

### Thesis use
State uncertainty-set factorization explicitly rather than referring generically to “robust MDP.”

### Citation
Nilim and El Ghaoui (2005), Sections 2.2 and 3.1.

## E3 — Stationary and time-varying uncertainty models are different
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 2.2
- **Claim:** The paper distinguishes an unknown transition model chosen once and then held fixed from a stronger model in which transition matrices may vary over time within the prescribed bounds.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
In the stationary uncertainty model, nature selects transition matrices that remain fixed for the horizon. In the time-varying model, admissible transition matrices may change between stages. The time-varying formulation is easier to solve with robust dynamic programming but represents a different uncertainty assumption.

### Thesis use
Do not equate time-varying robust uncertainty with an unknown piecewise-stationary changepoint process; state which temporal uncertainty model is being evaluated.

### Citation
Nilim and El Ghaoui (2005), Section 2.2.

## E4 — Statistical quality of the uncertainty set affects conservativeness and tractability
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction; Sections 5–7
- **Claim:** Some generic polytopic or interval uncertainty sets can be poor statistical representations and produce overly conservative robust policies; likelihood- or entropy-based sets can provide a more meaningful alternative in the paper's setting.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The authors argue that tractability alone is not enough when choosing an uncertainty set. Broad interval-style sets can include unrealistic transition models and induce excessive pessimism. They develop uncertainty models based on likelihood regions or entropy bounds that retain statistical meaning while keeping the inner robust recursion computationally manageable.

### Thesis use
Report uncertainty-set family, calibration procedure, radius, and nominal-performance loss together.

### Citation
Nilim and El Ghaoui (2005), Abstract, Introduction, and Sections 5–7.

## E5 — Robust planning is not online resilience
- **Type:** scope synthesis grounded in the formulation
- **Location:** Overall paper
- **Claim:** The robust policy is optimized for an uncertainty model; the paper does not define an unknown changepoint detector, context recall, or a continued-learning recovery process.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
Keep `static_robust_policy` and `adaptive_post_change_policy` as different agent categories and test out-of-set structural shifts separately from in-set model uncertainty.

### Citation
Nilim and El Ghaoui (2005), overall formulation.
