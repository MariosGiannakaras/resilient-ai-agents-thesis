# Evidence — Quantitative Resilience Modeling for Autonomous Cyber Defense

## E1 — Resilience is treated as a time-evolving recovery property

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Cover page contributions; Abstract; Section 1, pp. 894–895
- **Claim:** The paper treats resilience as recovery with limited operational impact and explicitly aims to capture temporal evolution during an attack.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Cadet et al. formulate cyber resilience as the ability of a system to recover from attack while limiting disruption to operations. Their contribution is explicitly time-dependent: the metric is intended to show how resilience changes as an attack progresses rather than merely assign one static label to a complete run.

### Context and limits

The definition is operationalized for cyber networks and adversarial attacks. It is evidence for temporal resilience reasoning, not a universal mathematical definition that overrides the thesis protocol.

### Thesis use

Support the separation of disturbance impact, subsequent recovery behavior, and final performance.

### Citation

Cadet et al. (2025), Abstract, contribution 1, and Section 1.

## E2 — Whole-run aggregation can erase recovery information

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4.2, p. 900
- **Claim:** A time interval equal to the duration of the entire game is too coarse because total impacts lose information about the recovery process; a single-step interval is also too fine-grained.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The authors make time granularity an explicit design issue. Measuring the entire game as one interval compresses the temporal structure of the disturbance and hides recovery behavior, while a one-step interval is too noisy and local for interpreting the overall attack pattern. Their analysis therefore preserves an intermediate time scale.

### Context and limits

The paper later uses a Gaussian rolling mean and reports a convenient `T/10` exploratory setting for its cyber experiments. That numerical choice is domain-specific and must not be imported into the thesis recovery windows.

### Thesis use

Motivate retaining fixed time-resolved recovery windows instead of replacing RQ3 with a whole-horizon aggregate.

### Citation

Cadet et al. (2025), Section 4.2.

## E3 — Normalization enables aggregation across attacks and topologies

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4.2, Equations 2–3, pp. 900–901; Section 6
- **Claim:** The paper normalizes resilience drop by a maximum possible interval drop so the metric can be aggregated and compared across network topologies and attack patterns.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

After defining a domain-specific resilience drop, the authors divide it by a maximum possible drop for the interval. This produces a normalized quantity used to compare defense behavior across different attacks and network layouts and to construct higher-level summaries over heterogeneous settings.

### Context and limits

The denominator is meaningful because the paper defines explicit cyber assets, operational goals, and impact costs. The thesis must not reuse this denominator mechanically because its disturbances and estimands are different.

### Thesis use

Support the general reporting principle that cross-condition aggregation requires a justified common scale while retaining condition-level trajectories.

### Citation

Cadet et al. (2025), Section 4.2, Equations 2–3, and Section 6.

## E4 — Average summaries can hide heterogeneous temporal patterns

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 6.1–6.3, pp. 904–905
- **Claim:** The paper supplements average resilience summaries with clustering of temporal resilience patterns because aggregate behavior can conceal subgroups with materially different degradation profiles.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

When many attack realizations are pooled, Cadet et al. do not rely only on an overall mean trajectory. They group resilience time series by shape and show that subsets of runs can display substantially different peak degradation and recovery patterns from the aggregate curve. This illustrates why an average can be informative yet incomplete.

### Context and limits

The specific cluster proportions and cyber attack magnitudes are properties of their experiments and are not transferable numerical evidence for the thesis.

### Thesis use

Motivate showing root-level or condition-level variation alongside aggregates and avoiding claims based only on a single mean curve.

### Citation

Cadet et al. (2025), Section 6.1–6.3.

## E5 — Operational resilience is distinct from ordinary RL cumulative return

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 1, pp. 895–896; Section 3; Section 4.1
- **Claim:** The authors note that RL cyber defenses are commonly evaluated by cumulative returns, whereas their resilience formulation separately represents operational impacts and recovery-oriented objectives.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The paper motivates its metric by observing that cumulative RL return does not by itself characterize how cyber operations absorb, experience, and recover from attacks. The resilience formulation instead tracks operational impacts over time and allows different operational objectives to receive explicit weights.

### Context and limits

The confidentiality, availability, integrity weights and asset costs are cyber-specific design choices. They should inform the conceptual distinction between task return and resilience, not be imported as thesis objectives.

### Thesis use

Support the thesis decision to report nominal performance, disturbance loss and temporal recovery as separate quantities rather than collapsing them into one score.

### Citation

Cadet et al. (2025), Sections 1, 3, and 4.1.
