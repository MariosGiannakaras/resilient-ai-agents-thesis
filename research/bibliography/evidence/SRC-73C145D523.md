# Evidence — Safe Reinforcement Learning with Dead-Ends Avoidance and Recovery

## E1 — Task performance and safety are explicitly decoupled
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction; Methods
- **Claim:** DEA-RRL uses a task policy for reward maximization and a separately trained recovery policy and safety critic for safety control.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Zhang et al. follow a decoupled safe-RL architecture in which the task policy need not optimize the safety objective directly. A recovery policy and its safety critic are pretrained with the goal of maximizing safety. During task learning, the critic evaluates proposed task actions and the controller can replace an unsafe proposal with a recovery action.

### Thesis use
Log task proposals, safety estimates, interventions, and executed actions as separate signals.

### Citation
Zhang et al. (2023), Abstract, Introduction, and Methods.

## E2 — Dead-end states are irrecoverable under the problem formulation
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section III-B, Definition 2
- **Claim:** A dead-end is a state from which eventual transition to failure is unavoidable regardless of which policy is followed.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper partitions the state space into failure, dead-end, and safe states. Dead-end states are defined so that no admissible policy can prevent eventual failure from those states, while a safe state has at least one action leading to another safe state. The authors use this distinction to motivate a boundary that excludes truly irrecoverable regions rather than every state that looks dangerous under a weak current policy.

### Thesis use
For structural changes, distinguish states that are currently hard for the task policy from states that have become genuinely unrecoverable.

### Citation
Zhang et al. (2023), Section III-B, Definition 2.

## E3 — Policy-dependent safety evaluation can create an overly conservative boundary
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Introduction; Section IV-A
- **Claim:** A safety critic that evaluates a poorly trained task policy may label recoverable states as unsafe because it measures the safety of that policy rather than the best achievable recovery behavior.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The authors argue that Recovery-RL-style evaluation of the task policy can overestimate risk early in training. If the task policy is poor in underexplored states, the critic can treat those states as unsafe even when a better recovery policy could act safely there. This shrinks the apparent safe exploration region and can reinforce under-exploration.

### Thesis use
Measure false-positive interventions and compare policy-dependent risk estimation with recovery-policy-based or oracle reachability diagnostics where feasible.

### Citation
Zhang et al. (2023), Introduction and Section IV-A.

## E4 — Recovery to safety is distinct from recovery of task performance
- **Type:** scope synthesis grounded in the architecture
- **Location:** Overall method
- **Claim:** The recovery policy is optimized to avoid failure and return the agent toward safer behavior; it does not itself restore the task policy's pre-change return.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
Report safety interventions and task-return recovery separately, especially after a topology or dynamics shift.

### Citation
Zhang et al. (2023), overall formulation.

## E5 — Offline pretraining is a prior-data advantage
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction; Methods
- **Claim:** The recovery policy and safety critic are pretrained on an offline dataset before online task-policy learning.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
DEA-RRL relies on offline experience to pretrain its safety machinery. The later online learner therefore begins with information about unsafe behavior that a pure-online baseline may not possess.

### Thesis use
Account for offline safety data, pretraining interactions, and pretraining compute when making fairness claims.

### Citation
Zhang et al. (2023), Abstract and Methods.

## E6 — A structural shift can change the recoverable set even if local hazard labels do not
- **Type:** thesis-protocol implication grounded in the dead-end definition
- **Location:** Section III-B
- **Claim:** Recoverability depends on transition structure and available safe continuations, not only on whether the current cell is locally hazardous.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
After blocked-edge, corridor, or transition changes, recompute or empirically reassess recoverability; a pre-change safety critic should not be assumed valid without revalidation.

### Citation
Zhang et al. (2023), Section III-B.
