# Evidence — Transfer Learning in Deep Reinforcement Learning: A Survey

## Evidence E1 — Continual learning is more than static source-to-target transfer
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 2.4, Continual Learning
- **Claim:** The survey describes continual learning as a more demanding transfer setting in which knowledge must be carried through a sequence of dynamically changing tasks that may not be known in advance.
- **Thesis use:** mechanism taxonomy
- **Topics:** continual learning; transfer; sequential tasks
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Standard transfer learning can focus on using information from one or more source domains to improve learning in a target domain. Continual learning instead requires an agent to learn sequentially across changing tasks while retaining useful knowledge from earlier tasks. The future task sequence may not be known beforehand.

## Evidence E2 — Automatic task detection and forgetting control are central continual-learning requirements
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 2.4, Continual Learning
- **Claim:** Automatic task detection and avoiding catastrophic forgetting are identified as particularly important in continual learning.
- **Thesis use:** recurring-context protocol
- **Topics:** task detection; catastrophic forgetting; context recall
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
When task boundaries are not supplied explicitly, a continual learner needs a way to identify which task or regime it is facing. It also needs to prevent new learning from destroying previously useful competence. These are separate requirements and should not be collapsed into a single context-memory mechanism.

## Evidence E3 — Negative transfer requires a no-transfer comparator
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 2.4, Multi-task RL; survey discussion of transfer challenges
- **Claim:** Sharing knowledge between irrelevant or conflicting tasks can reduce performance rather than improve it.
- **Thesis use:** baseline design
- **Topics:** negative transfer; no-transfer baseline; task similarity
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Knowledge transfer is not automatically beneficial. When tasks are poorly related or conflict, shared representations or policies can interfere with task-specific learning. A transfer or context-recall method therefore needs comparison against learning without transferred knowledge so that negative transfer is visible.

## Evidence E4 — Final target mastery and adaptation speed are different transfer outcomes
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 3.3, transfer-learning evaluation metrics
- **Claim:** Transfer evaluation can consider both how well the target task is ultimately mastered and how rapidly useful performance is reached.
- **Thesis use:** recovery metrics
- **Topics:** transfer efficiency; target performance; adaptation speed
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
A method can reach a strong final target policy but learn it slowly, or adapt quickly but plateau at a lower level. Evaluation should therefore keep post-change adaptation speed and final post-change performance as separate dimensions.

## Evidence E5 — Transferred knowledge can take multiple forms
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** survey categorization and Section 5
- **Claim:** Transfer methods can operate through different knowledge objects, including reward shaping, demonstrations, teacher policies, mappings, and reusable representations.
- **Thesis use:** mechanism labeling
- **Topics:** policy transfer; representation transfer; reward shaping
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Transfer learning in RL is not one mechanism. The information being transferred can be a policy, demonstrations, a representation, a task mapping, reward-shaping knowledge, or another learned structure. These mechanisms have different assumptions and should be described and ablated separately.

## Avoid overclaiming
Use this source to justify transfer/continual-learning distinctions and evaluation dimensions. It does not empirically prove that a particular context-recall method will outperform continual tabular learning in the thesis GridWorld.
