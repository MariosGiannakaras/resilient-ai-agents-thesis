# Evidence — A Baseline for Detecting Misclassified and Out-of-Distribution Examples

## E1 — Maximum softmax probability is a simple detection baseline

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Section 1
- **Claim:** Correct and in-distribution examples tend to receive larger maximum softmax probabilities than misclassified or out-of-distribution examples, providing a simple ranking score.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Hendrycks and Gimpel use the maximum predicted softmax probability as a baseline detector. Across their classification tasks, correctly classified and in-distribution examples tend to have higher maximum probabilities than errors and OOD inputs, so the score can support binary ranking even without a separate detector model.

### Context and limits

The paper presents this as a baseline to be surpassed, not as a complete uncertainty-quantification method. Its experiments concern classifiers, not transition or reward changepoints in RL.

### Thesis use

Use a softmax-style score only as a reference detector for learned observation representations, not as evidence of environment-change detection by itself.

### Citation

Hendrycks and Gimpel (2016), Abstract and Section 1.

## E2 — AUROC and AUPR evaluate score-based detectors without relying on raw accuracy

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 2
- **Claim:** Thresholded accuracy can be misleading under class imbalance; AUROC and AUPR provide complementary score-based detection metrics.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The paper uses AUROC because it summarizes ranking performance across thresholds and AUPR because precision–recall behavior is sensitive to the base rate of the positive class. It explicitly notes that AUPR changes with which class is designated positive.

### Context and limits

Neither metric selects an operational alarm threshold or captures temporal costs such as detection delay.

### Thesis use

If a detector is evaluated, supplement false-alarm and delay metrics with AUROC/AUPR when the detector emits a continuous score.

### Citation

Hendrycks and Gimpel (2016), Section 2.

## E3 — Softmax probability is not calibrated confidence

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Introduction; Tables 1–2
- **Claim:** A softmax output can be highly confident on incorrect or OOD inputs, so its numeric probability should not be interpreted directly as confidence in correctness.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The authors show that neural classifiers can assign large predicted probabilities to wrong and out-of-distribution examples. Although aggregate probability statistics can separate groups well enough to form a useful detector, an individual softmax probability has poor direct correspondence with true confidence.

### Context and limits

High AUROC does not imply probability calibration, reliable near-OOD behavior, or suitability as a changepoint probability.

### Thesis use

Keep ranking-based detection performance separate from calibration claims and from Bayesian or epistemic uncertainty estimates.

### Citation

Hendrycks and Gimpel (2016), Introduction and Tables 1–2.
