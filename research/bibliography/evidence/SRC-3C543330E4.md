# Evidence — The Complexity of Non-Stationary Reinforcement Learning

## E1 — A local MDP change can have a global computational effect

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Section 1
- **Claim:** Under SETH, even a very local modification of an MDP can require nearly full-scale computation to keep an approximately optimal solution up to date in the worst case.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Peng and Papadimitriou study repeated finite-horizon MDP updates and prove a conditional worst-case lower bound. Their construction changes only the parameters of one state–action pair, and even an update involving only two transition probabilities can require amortized computation almost proportional to the overall `S×A×H` problem size to maintain a constant-quality approximation.

### Context and limits

This is a fine-grained worst-case complexity result conditional on SETH. It is not a prediction of wall-clock cost for every structured or small GridWorld.

### Thesis use

Use it as theoretical motivation for measuring update cost and comparing incremental adaptation with reset/relearning rather than assuming local environmental changes imply cheap policy updates.

### Citation

Peng and Papadimitriou (2024), Abstract and Section 1.

## E2 — The lower bound concerns maintaining approximate value across a sequence of updates

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Theorem 1
- **Claim:** The main hardness result applies to algorithms that must keep an approximate optimal value across many locally modified MDP rounds.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Theorem 1 states that, unless SETH is false, no general algorithm can achieve amortized per-update time substantially below `(SAH)^(1-o(1))` while maintaining the required approximation over all rounds in the constructed non-stationary MDP sequence.

### Context and limits

The construction is adversarial and worst-case. Structured heuristics can still perform well on restricted environment families.

### Thesis use

Report computational adaptation cost as an empirical quantity instead of assuming theoretical hardness determines the ranking in the benchmark.

### Citation

Peng and Papadimitriou (2024), Theorem 1.

## E3 — The hardness is not restricted to one type of local parameter change

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Remarks 11–12
- **Claim:** The paper extends the lower-bound interpretation to maintaining approximately optimal policies and to restricted changes involving rewards or transitions.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The authors explain that related hardness holds when the maintained object is an approximately optimal policy rather than only a scalar value estimate, and that the construction can be adapted so local non-stationarity is expressed through rewards, transitions, or a restricted subset of states.

### Thesis use

Measure compute and recovery separately for reward changes and transition/rule changes instead of treating all non-stationarity as equally difficult.

### Citation

Peng and Papadimitriou (2024), Remarks 11–12.

## E4 — Some structured update types are computationally easier

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Theorem 13; Algorithm 1
- **Claim:** Adding new actions has a more favorable incremental-update structure than arbitrary modifications of existing MDP parameters.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The paper complements its negative result with a positive algorithm for a restricted form of change: adding actions. Its lazy update procedure can maintain an approximate solution with cost governed by the horizon and approximation tolerance rather than the full number of states.

### Context and limits

This favorable result relies on the monotone structure of action insertion and does not extend automatically to reward inversion, blocked paths, or arbitrary transition changes.

### Thesis use

Record change type independently from severity because different structural changes can have fundamentally different adaptation costs.

### Citation

Peng and Papadimitriou (2024), Theorem 13 and Algorithm 1.

## E5 — Restart and renewed exploration are practical implications, not proven universal winners

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 5, Discussion
- **Claim:** The authors interpret the lower bound as motivation for strategies that explore after change and sometimes restart rather than indefinitely attempting cheap local updates.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

In the discussion, the paper suggests that severe computational barriers to generic incremental maintenance make restart-style strategies and renewed exploration plausible practical responses when continued updating yields diminishing benefit.

### Context and limits

This is an implication discussed by the authors, not a theorem showing detector-triggered reset always outperforms continual learning.

### Thesis use

Include full reset and exploration-boost comparators, while measuring their actual recovery and knowledge-loss costs empirically.

### Citation

Peng and Papadimitriou (2024), Section 5.
