# Evidence — Safe Reinforcement Learning in Constrained Markov Decision Processes

## E1 — The safety function can be unknown and learned during safe exploration
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction; Section 2
- **Claim:** SNO-MDP studies an MDP in which both reward and a state-based safety function are initially unknown, while every visited state must satisfy a safety threshold.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Wachi and Sui formulate safe reinforcement learning with an unknown safety function `g(s)` and require the agent to remain in states whose safety value is above a threshold. The key difficulty is that the learner must collect information about safety without freely entering states whose safety has not been established.

### Context and limits
The theoretical results rely on prior safe states and regularity assumptions for the unknown functions, including kernel/RKHS and Lipschitz structure.

### Thesis use
Distinguish methods that know the hazard map in advance from methods that spend data and compute learning safety information.

### Citation
Wachi and Sui (2020), Abstract, Introduction, and Section 2.

## E2 — A pessimistic safe set includes reachability and returnability, not only local state labels
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 3, Predicted pessimistic safe space
- **Claim:** States admitted to the certified pessimistic safe region must be judged safe with high probability and also be reachable from and returnable to the previously certified region.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper first forms a set of states whose lower confidence bounds satisfy the safety threshold. It then restricts this set using reachability and returnability operators. A locally safe-looking state is therefore not enough: the agent must be able to enter it through an allowed transition and retain a safe route back to the established safe set.

### Thesis use
For blocked-corridor or topology changes, recompute safe connectivity rather than assuming unchanged per-cell hazard labels imply unchanged safety.

### Citation
Wachi and Sui (2020), Section 3.

## E3 — The optimistic and pessimistic safe sets serve different purposes
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 3, Predicted optimistic safe space
- **Claim:** The pessimistic set contains states certified safe at the chosen confidence level, whereas the optimistic set includes states that may still prove safe and can guide information gathering.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The optimistic set is built from upper confidence information and includes states that remain plausible candidates for safety, even when they are not yet certified by the lower bound. The pessimistic set is the region in which the safety guarantee is maintained. This creates an explicit distinction between states that are safe enough to use and states that are merely promising to investigate.

### Thesis use
Do not collapse `uncertain-but-potentially-safe` and `certified-safe` states into one label when evaluating safety exploration.

### Citation
Wachi and Sui (2020), Section 3.

## E4 — SNO-MDP separates safety exploration from reward optimization
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction; algorithm overview
- **Claim:** The algorithm first expands or learns the certified safe region and then optimizes cumulative reward within the safe region.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
SNO-MDP uses a stepwise procedure rather than optimizing reward and safety discovery as one undifferentiated objective. The first phase gathers enough information to construct an appropriate safe region. The second phase performs reward-oriented learning subject to the certified safety restriction.

### Context and limits
This separation incurs an opportunity cost because interactions spent learning safety are not directly optimized for task reward.

### Thesis use
Report safety-exploration steps, task-return opportunity cost, and the delay before reward-focused adaptation resumes.

### Citation
Wachi and Sui (2020), Abstract and Introduction.

## E5 — Early stopping limits unnecessary safety exploration
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Introduction; ES2 contribution and later algorithm analysis
- **Claim:** The ES2 mechanism is designed to stop safety-region exploration once sufficient information has been collected for near-optimal reward behavior, rather than expanding the safe region indefinitely.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The authors introduce Early Stopping of Exploration of Safety to reduce the amount of exploration devoted to safety while preserving probabilistic safety and near-optimality guarantees under their assumptions. The motivation is that complete knowledge of every potentially safe state is unnecessary when additional safety information would not materially improve the achievable reward policy.

### Thesis use
If a safety-learning baseline is included, distinguish information needed for safe task execution from exhaustive hazard-map learning.

### Citation
Wachi and Sui (2020), Introduction and ES2 analysis.

## E6 — The safety machinery is not a generic changepoint detector
- **Type:** scope synthesis grounded in the paper
- **Location:** Overall formulation
- **Claim:** SNO-MDP learns initially unknown reward and safety functions under a fixed structured model; it does not primarily detect temporal changes in the environment and then trigger post-shift relearning.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
Use SNO-MDP as background for unknown-safety exploration and safe-set connectivity, not as evidence that a GP safety model detects arbitrary environmental shifts.

### Citation
Wachi and Sui (2020), overall formulation.
