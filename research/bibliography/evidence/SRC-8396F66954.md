# Evidence — Concrete Problems in AI Safety

## Evidence E1 — Distributional shift is a distinct safety problem
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Sections 2 and 7
- **Claim:** Robustness to distributional shift concerns avoiding bad decisions when deployment inputs or environments differ materially from those seen during training.
- **Thesis use:** terminology; scope
- **Topics:** distributional shift; deployment; robustness
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper treats distributional shift as a learning-process failure mode in which a system encounters conditions that differ from its training distribution and can make harmful, silent, or unpredictable decisions. This is distinct from having specified the wrong objective in the first place.

## Evidence E2 — Safe exploration and adaptation performance must be measured separately
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 2 and 6
- **Claim:** Exploratory behavior can have serious or irreversible consequences even when exploration is valuable for long-term learning.
- **Thesis use:** safety/recovery metric separation
- **Topics:** safe exploration; irreversible harm; adaptation cost
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
An RL agent may need exploration to improve its policy, but some exploratory actions can impose costs that are unacceptable even if they eventually lead to better long-run performance. An adaptation mechanism can therefore recover task return while still being undesirable because of the safety cost incurred during exploration.

## Evidence E3 — Reward hacking is not the same as an externally changing reward function
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 2 and 4
- **Claim:** Reward hacking arises when the formal objective admits an unintended way to obtain high reward while violating the designer's intent.
- **Thesis use:** terminology boundary
- **Topics:** reward hacking; specification; non-stationarity
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The reward-hacking problem is caused by an objective that can be gamed: the agent finds behavior that formally scores well but defeats the intended purpose of the objective. This is conceptually different from a benchmark in which the external environment changes the reward function over time; the latter is a non-stationary reward scenario rather than reward hacking by definition.

## Evidence E4 — Task success can coexist with harmful side effects
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 2–3
- **Claim:** An agent can optimize its main objective while causing undesirable changes to parts of the environment that the objective does not represent.
- **Thesis use:** multi-metric evaluation
- **Topics:** side effects; return; environmental cost
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
A narrowly specified task objective can make the agent indifferent to other environmental variables. The resulting policy can achieve the target task efficiently while causing collateral changes that humans regard as harmful. Task return and side-effect or safety cost should therefore not be hidden inside one opaque score.

## Evidence E5 — Greater autonomy increases the importance of measuring online harm
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 2
- **Claim:** Systems that act directly on the world can cause harms that are harder for humans to intercept than errors in systems that only provide recommendations.
- **Thesis use:** motivation; adaptation-period logging
- **Topics:** autonomy; intervention; online harm
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
As systems receive more direct control over real processes, harmful actions may occur before a human can correct them. For an online adaptation experiment, this supports measuring the costs incurred during the adaptation period rather than reporting only the final post-adaptation policy.

## Evidence E6 — The paper is a research-problem taxonomy, not an algorithm ranking
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction; concluding discussion
- **Claim:** The paper organizes practical safety research problems and proposes experimental directions rather than providing a unified empirical ranking of safety algorithms.
- **Thesis use:** claim boundary
- **Topics:** taxonomy; evidence scope
- **Status:** verified

## Avoid overclaiming
Use this source to define failure modes and reporting boundaries. Do not cite it as evidence that a particular resilience algorithm is safer or more effective than another.
