# Evidence — Policy Consolidation for Continual Reinforcement Learning

## Evidence E1 — Continual consolidation can operate without explicit task boundaries
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Section 1
- **Claim:** Policy Consolidation is designed to operate continuously, without requiring a signal that identifies task boundaries or the timescale of distribution changes.
- **Thesis use:** mechanism taxonomy
- **Topics:** task boundaries; continual learning; non-stationarity
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The method continually stores and regularizes the current policy against a cascade of historical policies that evolve at different timescales. Consolidation is therefore always active rather than being triggered by a supplied task-boundary label.

### Limitation
Boundary-free regularization is not equivalent to calibrated changepoint detection; the method does not claim to identify when a change occurred.

## Evidence E2 — Behavioral retention should be measured in policy space, not only parameter space
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 3.1–3.2
- **Claim:** The paper replaces parameter-space distance with KL divergence between action distributions because nearby parameters need not imply similar behavior.
- **Thesis use:** retention metrics
- **Topics:** policy distance; KL divergence; behavioral memory
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The authors motivate policy-level consolidation by noting that a neural network's parameter distance is an unreliable proxy for how similarly two policies act. Their consolidation objective therefore constrains adjacent historical policies using KL divergence between their action distributions.

### Thesis-safe implication
For a tabular agent, an analogous retention diagnostic can compare actions or value-induced decisions on a shared probe-state set rather than relying only on raw parameter/Q-table distance.

## Evidence E3 — Non-stationarity can arise from different mechanisms
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 1, 2.4 and 4
- **Claim:** The experiments cover alternating tasks, within-task policy-induced distribution change, and competitive self-play, which represent different forms of non-stationarity.
- **Thesis use:** scenario taxonomy
- **Topics:** endogenous non-stationarity; exogenous task switch; self-play
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper studies several changing-data regimes: explicit alternation between tasks, a single task in which the agent's evolving policy changes its own experience distribution, and self-play in which an opponent's evolving policy changes the effective environment. These sources of change should not be treated as identical.

### Limitation
Multi-agent policy-induced non-stationarity is not the same as an exogenous environmental shift in a single-agent GridWorld.

## Evidence E4 — Policy Consolidation improves continual retention in the reported deep-PPO settings
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4, including self-play experiments
- **Claim:** The paper reports improved continual-learning behavior relative to its PPO baselines across the evaluated settings, including reduced forgetting against historical policies.
- **Thesis use:** background/feasibility evidence
- **Topics:** forgetting; historical policies; continual PPO
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
In the reported continuous-control and self-play experiments, the Policy Consolidation agent generally preserves competence more effectively than the compared PPO baselines. In self-play, later consolidated policies remain competitive against a broad range of their historical versions, whereas several baseline policies exhibit instability or regression.

### Limitation
This result is tied to the deep continuous-control/PPO setup and does not establish an ordering for tabular Q-learning agents.

## Evidence E5 — Stability can trade off against plasticity
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** self-play discussion and limitations
- **Claim:** Strong consolidation can slow initial learning because the current policy is constrained too strongly by its history.
- **Thesis use:** stability-plasticity trade-off
- **Topics:** retention; adaptation speed; over-consolidation
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The authors note that an initially strong influence from older policies can make the consolidated agent learn more slowly. Preserving prior behavior can therefore improve retention while simultaneously delaying adaptation to new experience.

### Thesis-safe implication
Backward retention and time-to-recovery should be reported separately; high retention is not by itself evidence of high resilience.

## Avoid overclaiming
Policy Consolidation is a boundary-free continual regularizer, not a changepoint detector. Its neural/PPO implementation is background evidence rather than a required tabular baseline for the thesis.
