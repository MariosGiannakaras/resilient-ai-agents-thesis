# Evidence — State Entropy Regularization for Robust Reinforcement Learning

## E1 — State entropy and policy entropy encourage different kinds of diversity
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction
- **Claim:** Policy-entropy regularization encourages stochastic action selection, whereas state-entropy regularization directly encourages broader coverage of the induced state-visitation distribution.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Ashlag et al. distinguish randomness over actions from diversity over visited states. Policy entropy can spread action probability while leaving behavior concentrated around a dominant trajectory. State entropy instead rewards policies whose discounted occupancy covers a broader portion of state space, which can preserve alternative high-value routes.

### Thesis use
Report action entropy and state-visitation coverage as separate diagnostics; neither should be used as a proxy for the other.

### Citation
Ashlag et al. (2025), Abstract and Introduction.

## E2 — State entropy is especially relevant to structured, spatially correlated perturbations
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction; Section 5 overview
- **Claim:** The paper finds theoretical and empirical robustness benefits for structured perturbations that disrupt spatially concentrated behavior, such as obstacles placed along a dominant path.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The motivating case is a policy whose nominal behavior repeatedly uses one high-reward route. A localized obstacle can invalidate that route even when policy entropy is high. By encouraging occupancy over multiple regions or near-optimal trajectories, state-entropy regularization can retain useful alternatives under such spatially correlated changes.

### Thesis use
Use state-visitation coverage as an optional ablation for wall, blockage, or topology perturbations in GridWorld, not as a generic solution to every kind of uncertainty.

### Citation
Ashlag et al. (2025), Abstract, Introduction, and Section 5 overview.

## E3 — Regularization strength determines the induced reward-robustness set and conservativeness
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 3.1; Theorems 3.1–3.2
- **Claim:** State-entropy regularization can be interpreted through a reward-robust objective whose uncertainty set changes systematically with the regularization coefficient.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper derives a dual robust-RL interpretation in which state-entropy regularization corresponds to optimization against an explicit family of reward perturbations. Increasing the temperature/regularization parameter changes the geometry and size of the induced uncertainty set, so the amount and type of conservatism are part of the algorithm specification rather than incidental side effects.

### Thesis use
If an entropy-regularized comparator is used, sweep and report the regularization coefficient and include nominal-return cost alongside disturbed performance.

### Citation
Ashlag et al. (2025), Section 3.1 and Theorems 3.1–3.2.

## E4 — Entropy regularization is not a general solution to transition-kernel robustness
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Contributions; Section 4 overview
- **Claim:** The paper explicitly proves limitations: state, policy, or joint entropy regularization does not in general solve arbitrary kernel-robust RL problems and can perform poorly under some risk-sensitive criteria.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Although state entropy yields nontrivial robustness bounds in some transition-uncertainty settings, the authors show that entropy regularization cannot be treated as an exact generic solution to kernel-robust RL. They also construct settings in which entropy regularization can arbitrarily hurt risk-averse performance.

### Thesis use
Do not equate broad exploration or occupancy diversity with a universal robustness or resilience guarantee.

### Citation
Ashlag et al. (2025), Contributions and Section 4 overview.

## E5 — Reported robustness benefits depend on the rollout budget used for policy evaluation
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Contributions; Sections 4.3 and 5 overview
- **Claim:** The empirical advantage of state-entropy regularization is more sensitive than policy entropy to the number of rollouts available for policy evaluation.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper reports that state-entropy robustness gains diminish in low-sample evaluation regimes and explicitly identifies rollout count as a practical sensitivity. This means the apparent benefit can change with the evaluation or planning sample budget even when the underlying algorithmic objective is unchanged.

### Thesis use
Keep rollout/evaluation budgets equal across comparators and report them as resource variables.

### Citation
Ashlag et al. (2025), Abstract, Contributions, Sections 4.3 and 5 overview.

## E6 — This is a robustness/coverage mechanism, not a post-change adaptation mechanism
- **Type:** scope synthesis grounded in the paper
- **Location:** Overall formulation
- **Claim:** The method changes the training objective so the learned policy has broader coverage and specified robustness properties; it does not add a changepoint detector, regime memory, or an explicit post-shift recovery update rule.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
Keep `entropy_regularized_robustness` separate from `change_detection`, `context_recall`, and `online_relearning` in the agent taxonomy.

### Citation
Ashlag et al. (2025), overall formulation.
