# Evidence — Benchmark for Out-of-Distribution Detection in Deep Reinforcement Learning

## E1 — RL-specific OOD evaluation needs environment-level shifts
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Sections 1–2
- **Claim:** The paper motivates an OOD benchmark for reinforcement-learning policies because most established OOD evaluations come from static supervised classification rather than agent–environment interaction.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Mohammed and Valdenegro-Toro argue that RL policies can encounter deployment conditions not represented in training, but the available OOD-detection literature and benchmarks are dominated by image-classification settings. Their benchmark therefore constructs altered RL environments so uncertainty methods can be evaluated on observations generated under changed environment conditions.

### Thesis use
Evaluate detectors on controlled environment perturbations rather than only injecting arbitrary anomalies into a static feature dataset.

### Citation
Mohammed and Valdenegro-Toro (2021), Abstract and Sections 1–2.

## E2 — Dynamics changes and observation corruptions are different OOD mechanisms
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4; Table 1
- **Claim:** The benchmark generates OOD conditions either by changing physical environment parameters or by corrupting the observation channel.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
For CartPole and Pendulum, the authors alter physical parameters such as gravity, masses, lengths, force, speed, and torque. For Pong, they instead corrupt visual observations using noise, blur, and related image corruptions. These two procedures modify different parts of the agent–environment system even though both are used to construct OOD test conditions.

### Thesis use
Keep `transition/dynamics_shift` and `observation_corruption` as separate perturbation categories and report their detector results separately.

### Citation
Mohammed and Valdenegro-Toro (2021), Section 4 and Table 1.

## E3 — OOD performance should be measured across perturbation severity
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4; benchmark parameter grids
- **Claim:** The benchmark evaluates multiple magnitudes of physical-parameter change and several severity levels of observation corruption.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Rather than defining one binary altered environment, the benchmark sweeps parameter values away from their defaults and uses several corruption strengths. This allows detector behavior to be examined as the test condition moves farther from training conditions.

### Thesis use
Report detection quality, false alarms, missed changes, and recovery behavior as functions of shift severity rather than only at one hand-picked perturbation level.

### Citation
Mohammed and Valdenegro-Toro (2021), Section 4.

## E4 — Ensembles performed best among the three uncertainty baselines tested here
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; experimental results
- **Claim:** In the reported benchmark, ensembles had stronger OOD-detection performance and lower variability across environments than the tested MC Dropout and MC DropConnect methods.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The study compares Monte Carlo Dropout, Monte Carlo DropConnect, and independently initialized model ensembles as uncertainty estimators. Within these experiments, the ensemble approach provides the strongest overall OOD-detection results and lower standard deviation across several benchmark environments.

### Context and limits
This is a benchmark-specific empirical ordering among three neural uncertainty methods. It does not establish that ensembles are universally optimal, especially for tabular agents or sequential changepoint detection.

### Thesis use
Treat ensembles as optional neural UQ background, not a required core detector.

### Citation
Mohammed and Valdenegro-Toro (2021), Abstract and results.

## E5 — Static OOD discrimination is not an online changepoint detector
- **Type:** scope inference grounded in the benchmark
- **Location:** Experimental protocol
- **Claim:** The benchmark distinguishes in-distribution from OOD conditions but does not evaluate detection delay, false-alarm processes, or missed changepoints in a piecewise-stationary online sequence.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper evaluates whether uncertainty methods assign distinguishable scores to data produced by ordinary versus modified environments. It does not define a temporal decision rule that observes a stream, declares a change at an estimated time, and is scored for how early or falsely it triggers.

### Thesis use
A detector inspired by OOD uncertainty must still be calibrated and evaluated with false-alarm rate, detection delay, missed-change rate, and threshold-selection protocol.

### Citation
Mohammed and Valdenegro-Toro (2021), benchmark methodology.

## E6 — OOD detection does not imply adaptation
- **Type:** thesis-scope synthesis
- **Location:** Overall paper
- **Claim:** Detecting that an observation or environment is outside the training distribution does not specify how the policy should adapt afterward.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
Keep `detector_score`, `change_decision`, and `adaptation_response` as three separate components in the architecture and evaluation.

### Citation
Mohammed and Valdenegro-Toro (2021), overall scope.
