# Evidence — Reactive Exploration to Cope with Non-Stationarity in Lifelong Reinforcement Learning

## E1 — Reward, observation, and dynamics changes are distinct sources of non-stationarity

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 1 and 3; Figure 1
- **Claim:** The paper treats changes to rewards, observations, and transition dynamics as different non-stationary mechanisms with different adaptation challenges.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Steinparz et al. distinguish lifelong-RL shifts in the reward function, observation distribution, and environment dynamics. Their examples show that these mechanisms change different parts of the agent–environment interaction and can require different responses.

### Thesis use

Keep reward shifts, observation corruption/representation shifts, and transition/rule changes as separate scenario families before testing combined perturbations.

### Citation

Steinparz et al. (2022), Sections 1 and 3, Figure 1.

## E2 — Abruptness and change frequency are independent experimental axes

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 5.1
- **Claim:** The reward-shift experiments vary whether changes are abrupt or gradual and also vary the interval between repeated changes.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The study repeatedly changes the reward function under both abrupt and gradual schedules and uses different intervals between changes. This separates how quickly a regime changes from how often the agent encounters another change.

### Context and limits

The paper's numerical step counts are tied to its own training horizon and should not be copied directly into a smaller benchmark.

### Thesis use

Use a factorial design such as `change_mode × change_interval × severity`, scaling intervals relative to the learning horizon.

### Citation

Steinparz et al. (2022), Section 5.1.

## E3 — Exploration decay and stale replay can impede adaptation

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 5.2–5.3; Figures 3–4
- **Claim:** In the paper's DQN experiments, poor recovery is linked to diminished exploration and replay data from the previous regime.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Vanilla DQN adapts poorly after the studied reward shifts. The authors show that changing replay-buffer management and resetting exploration at known changepoints can substantially improve adaptation, which indicates that stale experience and low post-change exploration contribute to the observed failure.

### Context and limits

The exploration reset uses prior knowledge of when changes occur and is therefore an oracle-assisted intervention.

### Thesis use

Include simple recency-aware and exploration-reset baselines and label oracle changepoint information explicitly.

### Citation

Steinparz et al. (2022), Sections 5.2–5.3, Figures 3–4.

## E4 — Recall of an old context is not evidence of adaptation to the intervening context

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 5.4; Figure 5
- **Claim:** An agent can fail in a changed context and then immediately regain performance when the original context returns, demonstrating retention without successful adaptation.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

In the Color-Swap experiment, PPO performs poorly after the observation shift but quickly returns to its previous behavior when the original environment representation is restored. The result indicates that knowledge of the original context was retained even though the policy did not successfully adapt to the changed one.

### Thesis use

Report novel-context adaptation and known-context recall as separate capabilities.

### Citation

Steinparz et al. (2022), Section 5.4, Figure 5.

## E5 — Prediction error can be change-sensitive without being a calibrated detector

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 5.5; Figures 5–6
- **Claim:** Reward-model and forward-model errors can rise when the corresponding environment component changes and can be used to increase exploration.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Reactive Exploration uses prediction losses as intrinsic signals: reward-model error responds to changed reward contingencies and forward-model error can respond to altered observations or dynamics. Adding these signals to exploration improves adaptation in several studied shifts.

### Context and limits

Prediction error can also rise because of stochasticity, model misspecification, or optimization error. It is therefore not a calibrated changepoint probability by itself.

### Thesis use

Treat prediction error as a candidate change score and measure false triggers before using it to control exploration.

### Citation

Steinparz et al. (2022), Section 5.5, Figures 5–6.

## E6 — Adaptation strength has its own utility trade-off

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 5.5; Appendix B.1.2
- **Claim:** Too little intrinsic-reward weight gives insufficient reactive exploration, while too much can dominate the extrinsic task objective.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The paper's sensitivity analysis shows that the intrinsic signal must be balanced against task reward. Weak weighting may not change exploration enough after a shift, whereas strong weighting can make novelty-seeking overwhelm the extrinsic objective.

### Thesis use

Report nominal utility and sensitivity to adaptation strength, and tune this parameter only on validation regimes.

### Citation

Steinparz et al. (2022), Section 5.5 and Appendix B.1.2.
