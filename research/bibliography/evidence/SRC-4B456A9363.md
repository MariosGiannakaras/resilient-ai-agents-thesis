# Evidence — Non-stationary Reinforcement Learning under General Function Approximation

## E1 — Sliding windows reduce mismatch between stale samples and the current MDP

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Section 1
- **Claim:** SW-OPEA constructs its confidence set from recent episodes and accounts for local reward/transition variation so current-value estimates are not dominated by obsolete data.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Feng et al. design a confidence-set model-free learner whose non-stationary component is a sliding window. The confidence construction incorporates local variation across the episodes retained in that window so the current optimal value function remains plausible despite distribution mismatch between earlier and current MDPs.

### Context and limits

The theoretical method uses general function approximation and is substantially more complex than a simple tabular recency heuristic.

### Thesis use

Use the source as justification for a recent-memory baseline, not as a requirement to reproduce SW-OPEA itself.

### Citation

Feng et al. (2023), Abstract and Section 1.

## E2 — Sliding window, restart, and exponential weighting are different non-stationarity mechanisms

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 1.1
- **Claim:** The related literature handles stale data through finite windows, learner restarts, or gradual exponential down-weighting of older experience.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The paper distinguishes several algorithmic families: sliding-window approaches in tabular non-stationary MDPs, restart schemes for accumulated change, and exponentially weighted least-squares methods that continuously reduce the influence of older observations.

### Context and limits

These methods are developed under different structural assumptions and regret analyses and should not be treated as interchangeable.

### Thesis use

Retain separate baselines for full reset, finite recent window, and exponential recency/decay.

### Citation

Feng et al. (2023), Section 1.1.

## E3 — Window length creates a bias–uncertainty trade-off

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Theorem 5.2; Corollary 5.3
- **Claim:** The regret bound depends jointly on window length and environmental variation, so no single window is optimal for all rates of change.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

A shorter window reduces contamination from older regimes but uses fewer samples for estimation, whereas a longer window provides more data while increasing mismatch when rewards or transitions have changed. The theoretical choice of window length is tied to variation quantities in the environment.

### Context and limits

The theory does not provide a universal window size for a new GridWorld, and a budget-aware choice can itself be oracle-like if the true variation is assumed known.

### Thesis use

Tune effective memory length on validation shifts and report sensitivity rather than selecting it from final test results.

### Citation

Feng et al. (2023), Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.3.

## E4 — Dynamic regret contains both stationary-learning and non-stationarity costs

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Discussion following Corollary 5.3
- **Claim:** The theoretical bound separates a component associated with learning a stationary problem from additional terms caused by changes in rewards and transitions.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The analysis decomposes the dynamic-regret guarantee into a term analogous to the cost of learning a static MDP and extra penalties introduced by non-stationary reward and transition variation. This makes the adaptation burden conceptually distinct from baseline learning difficulty.

### Thesis use

Report clean learning performance separately from immediate degradation, recovery deficit, and adaptation overhead.

### Citation

Feng et al. (2023), discussion after Corollary 5.3.

## E5 — SW-OPEA is most attractive in relatively small-variation regimes

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 5–6
- **Claim:** The paper's theoretical comparisons indicate that SW-OPEA improves several dependencies but is principally advantageous when environmental variation is not too large.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

In linear and tabular special cases, the derived bound improves some dimension and horizon dependencies while having a less favorable dependence on average transition variation. The authors therefore characterize the method as efficient when the non-stationarity budget is comparatively small.

### Context and limits

These are comparisons of theoretical upper bounds, not empirical rankings from a common benchmark.

### Thesis use

Use recency-based baselines primarily for gradual or moderate change and retain reset-style comparators for severe abrupt shifts.

### Citation

Feng et al. (2023), Sections 5–6.
