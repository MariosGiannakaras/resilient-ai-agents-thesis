# Scientific analysis — A Survey of Reinforcement Learning Algorithms for Dynamically Varying Environments

## Bibliographic identity

Sindhu Padakandla, **A Survey of Reinforcement Learning Algorithms for Dynamically Varying Environments**, *ACM Computing Surveys*, 54(6), 2021. DOI: 10.1145/3459991. Preprint: arXiv:2005.10619.

- **Type:** focused survey of single-agent RL under changing environment models.
- **Checked original:** full arXiv paper; publication metadata cross-checked with the canonical record.
- **Thesis role:** focused non-stationary-RL framing and assumptions; complementary to the broader continual-RL review.

## Stationarity as an explicit assumption boundary

The survey sets up classical RL using an MDP and explicitly lists stationarity of the transition and reward functions as an assumption supporting the standard convergence framing. It then defines dynamically varying/non-stationary environments as settings in which this stationarity assumption no longer holds.

This is directly useful to the thesis because it prevents a common theoretical error: convergence/results established for stationary RL cannot be silently reused as guarantees for post-change tracking. The thesis's empirical experiment deliberately violates a stationary deployment assumption after a hidden boundary, so resistance and adaptation must be measured rather than inferred.

## Dynamically varying environments

The survey models a changing environment as a sequence/family of contexts or MDPs sharing state/action structure while transition and/or reward behavior may vary. It emphasizes that autonomous agents in such settings need to react or adapt to varying operating conditions.

The thesis uses a narrower and more controlled formulation. Its primary remap conditions change the relationship between intended and executed actions, while supporting diagnostics introduce action-execution failure or observation corruption. These are not all identical to the survey's simplest P/R-changing MDP notation, so the source should be used for the general stationarity/adaptation framing rather than as an exact formal description of every thesis disturbance.

## Model-free, model-based and context/change mechanisms

The survey reviews families of approaches rather than endorsing one universal solution. Its scope includes model-based/model-free methods and approaches related to context detection, continual learning and meta-learning. This supports the thesis's decision to compare mechanism-distinct strategies instead of treating “adaptive RL” as one homogeneous class.

The five scientific strategies intentionally remain much simpler than the full survey taxonomy:

- Fixed Q-Learning: frozen nominal control baseline;
- Adaptive Q-Learning: continued off-policy model-free updates;
- SARSA: continued on-policy model-free updates;
- Dyna-Q: learned-model planning without explicit recency bonus;
- Dyna-Q+: learned-model planning plus directed re-exploration.

The thesis should state that this is a bounded decomposition of mechanisms relevant to the research question, not a survey-complete benchmark.

## Why tracking cost matters

The survey frames the objective in dynamic settings partly in terms of reducing reward lost while the agent learns/adapts, rather than considering only the eventual policy after the change. This aligns with the thesis's emphasis on post-change trajectory metrics. A strategy that eventually reaches a similar terminal return can still incur a materially different cumulative deficit or immediate degradation while adapting.

The thesis metrics are more specifically defined elsewhere and should not be attributed verbatim to Padakandla. The source supports the general need to evaluate learning/adaptation cost under changing operating conditions.

## Experimental-design relevance

The survey's central distinction between stationary and non-stationary settings reinforces several protocol safeguards:

1. environmental changes must be explicit and reproducible;
2. agents must receive the same online information boundary for fair comparison;
3. configuration selection must happen on non-final data before held-out final outcomes;
4. final claims should be tied to measured performance under the declared change process, not to stationary convergence theory.

## Χρήση στη διπλωματική

This source can support:

1. Classical RL commonly relies on a stationarity assumption for transition/reward behavior in standard convergence formulations.
2. Realistic operating environments can violate this assumption, motivating RL methods that react or adapt to change.
3. Dynamically varying/non-stationary RL is a broad problem with multiple solution families, including context/change-aware, continual and model-based/model-free approaches.
4. Performance in changing environments should account for reward/performance lost during adaptation, not merely the final policy in isolation.
5. Stationary convergence results should not be cited as if they were non-stationary tracking guarantees.

## Limitations

- This is a survey, not primary empirical evidence for the five thesis strategies.
- Its main formalization emphasizes changing transition/reward models; observation corruption and intended-versus-executed action remapping require the thesis's own explicit environment contracts.
- The survey does not prescribe the thesis's severities, layout design, paired-root protocol or exact metrics.
- The reviewed literature spans settings and assumptions that are not directly comparable; no universal ranking should be inferred.
