# Scientific analysis — Towards Continual Reinforcement Learning: A Review and Perspectives

## Bibliographic identity

Khimya Khetarpal, Matthew Riemer, Irina Rish, Doina Precup, **Towards Continual Reinforcement Learning: A Review and Perspectives**, *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research*, 75, 1401–1476, 2022. DOI: 10.1613/JAIR.1.13673.

- **Type:** broad peer-reviewed continual-RL review.
- **Checked original:** arXiv v2 full-text HTML corresponding to the JAIR work; publication metadata cross-checked with JAIR/IBM/DBLP records.
- **Thesis role:** high-level problem formulation, taxonomy and evaluation rationale; not an algorithm-specific performance source.

## Continual RL framing

The review treats continual RL as the broad family that includes lifelong and non-stationary RL formulations. It emphasizes agents that learn online and incrementally, retain useful prior competence, adapt efficiently when circumstances change, and may operate without clean task boundaries.

This is a strong fit for the thesis's conceptual framing: the scientific subject is not GridWorld itself, but **agent strategies under uncertainty and environmental change**. A small GridWorld can be justified as a controlled testbed because it allows the exact source, timing and magnitude of non-stationarity to be manipulated while maintaining inspectable trajectories and reproducible experimental contracts.

The review also makes clear that continual RL is broader than the thesis experiment. The thesis should therefore present its five tabular strategies as a deliberately bounded mechanism study, not as comprehensive coverage of continual RL.

## Scope and driver of non-stationarity

A central contribution is the taxonomy of continual-RL formulations along two dimensions: the **scope** of non-stationarity and the **driver** of non-stationarity. The paper also discusses time dependence in transition dynamics, rewards, observations and action availability.

For the thesis this supports explicit disturbance semantics rather than the vague label “dynamic environment.” The protocol's separate action-remap, action-execution-failure and observation-corruption conditions can be described as controlled perturbations of distinct parts of the agent–environment interaction. The hidden change boundary is then an experimental device for testing adaptation without privileged changepoint information.

## Stability–plasticity tension

The review highlights the stability–plasticity dilemma: continual learners must balance the retention of useful prior knowledge against sufficient plasticity to respond to recent experience. This is directly relevant to the thesis's five-strategy ladder:

- Fixed Q-Learning maximizes stability of the learned nominal values by disabling post-change learning, but has no learning-based plasticity after change.
- Adaptive Q-Learning and SARSA preserve a simple tabular representation while continuing model-free updates.
- Dyna-Q adds model-based planning, potentially propagating new information more broadly but also relying on a learned model that can become stale.
- Dyna-Q+ adds directed re-exploration to obtain evidence about parts of the environment that may no longer match old expectations.

This mapping is a thesis interpretation of mechanism families; the review does not rank these five specific algorithms.

## Evaluation implications

Section 6 is especially important. The review states that accumulated reward is a strong performance indicator but may be insufficient by itself for understanding a continual learner. It argues for evaluation that probes different capabilities and carefully controls the degree/nature of non-stationarity.

This supports the thesis decision not to collapse resilience into one unlabeled composite score. The predeclared measurements instead separate:

- immediate degradation after change;
- cumulative post-change deficit;
- terminal/post-change performance;
- recovery as a secondary/sensitivity dimension;
- distributions, paired effects and uncertainty across roots/layouts.

The exact metrics above are thesis-defined and require their own methodological/evidence support; this review supports the general principle that continual-agent evaluation should be multidimensional and tied to a controlled non-stationarity design.

## Benchmark/testbed implications

The review notes that continual-RL studies frequently customize environments for the scientific question and warns that task design can introduce bias. It argues for principled generation of non-stationarity and carefully designed experiments.

For this thesis, that supports two safeguards:

1. GridWorld conditions/layouts/seeds are versioned and frozen before final outcomes rather than adjusted after seeing favorable results.
2. The five agents share the same information boundary, root-seed pairing, environment conditions and evaluation budget so that mechanism comparisons remain interpretable.

## Χρήση στη διπλωματική

This source can support:

1. Continual/lifelong/non-stationary RL concerns incremental agents operating under change, often without perfectly clean task boundaries.
2. Non-stationarity can be characterized by its scope and driver and may affect different environment components.
3. Stability versus plasticity is a central continual-learning tension.
4. Accumulated reward alone may not characterize all relevant continual-learning abilities.
5. Evaluation should control the nature/degree of non-stationarity and use carefully designed benchmarks/metrics.
6. Planning is one of several capabilities worth probing in continual RL, but the review does not establish a universal model-based advantage.

## Limitations and threats to misuse

- This is a review/taxonomy, not a controlled head-to-head empirical study of the five thesis strategies.
- Much of the broader continual-learning literature involves neural/function-approximation settings unlike the thesis's tabular mechanism study.
- The review does not prescribe the thesis's exact GridWorld, condition severities, metrics or sample size.
- Its taxonomy should not be presented as the only possible taxonomy of non-stationary RL.
- General statements about desirable continual-learning properties should not be converted into claims that any thesis agent satisfies them without empirical evidence.
