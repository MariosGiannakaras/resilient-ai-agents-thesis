# Evidence — Meta-Gradients in Non-Stationary Environments

## E1 — Context can drive online adaptation of learning hyperparameters

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 1 and 3
- **Claim:** Contextual meta-gradients replace a fixed meta-parameter with a learned function of selected statistics that can signal changes in the task or agent performance.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Luketina et al. let a meta-parameter be predicted from contextual features such as reward statistics, temporal-difference errors, values, or task beliefs. When the learning process has repeatable structure, the learned context-to-hyperparameter function can associate a performance change with a useful response, such as increasing exploration after a task switch.

### Context and limits

The context is not a ground-truth changepoint label. A reward drop may also be caused by stochastic variation, so context-triggered adaptation can overreact if the selected statistics are not specific enough.

### Thesis use

A lightweight adaptive-exploration baseline may change epsilon or another exploration parameter from a documented context signal, but false-trigger behavior must be measured.

### Citation

Luketina et al. (2022), Sections 1 and 3.

## E2 — Small gridworlds can isolate reward-only and reward-plus-transition switches

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4; Appendix A.2
- **Claim:** The paper uses a single stream of interaction in controlled gridworlds where either the reward mapping alone or both rewards and transitions change regularly.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

In Two Colors, a 5×5 gridworld periodically changes which object produces positive reward, creating a clear reward-rule switch. In Switching MDPs, the agent moves through a sequence of gridworlds whose reward and transition functions are randomly generated, so both the objective and environment dynamics can change. The agent experiences these changes in one continuous learning stream rather than restarting training for each task.

### Context and limits

The changes are discrete and regular by design. They do not represent the full range of unpredictable real-world non-stationarity.

### Thesis use

Maintain separate reward-only and combined reward/transition shift scenarios.

### Citation

Luketina et al. (2022), Section 4 and Appendix A.2.

## E3 — Contextual meta-gradients can accelerate post-switch learning in the studied environments

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 6, experiments on Two Colors and Switching MDPs
- **Claim:** Providing informative context makes meta-parameter schedules react more quickly to task switches and improves lifetime performance relative to context-free variants in the reported settings.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

In Two Colors, context-free meta-gradients tend toward nearly constant schedules, whereas reward-context variants learn exploration schedules that change when performance drops after a switch. In Switching MDPs, contextual information also improves performance, including in the large task set where exact tasks are unlikely to repeat.

### Context and limits

The effect depends on the selected context features, meta-parameterization, tuning, and regular task-change process. The paper does not provide a universal post-change recovery-time guarantee.

### Thesis use

If an adaptive exploration mechanism is tested, report explicit recovery metrics instead of relying only on lifetime return.

### Citation

Luketina et al. (2022), Section 6.

## E4 — Non-stationarity has a rate beyond which adaptation cannot keep up

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 6.4 and corresponding appendix experiments
- **Claim:** As task switches become more frequent, the advantage of adaptive meta-gradient methods can shrink or disappear because the agent receives too little stationary interaction to learn effectively.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The experiments vary the interval between environment changes. Rich-context methods remain useful across a broader range of switch frequencies than context-free methods, but sufficiently rapid switching eventually prevents meaningful learning and removes the relative performance advantage.

### Context and limits

The numerical switch intervals are specific to the study. They should not be reused as universal thresholds.

### Thesis use

Treat change frequency as an experimental factor, include an intentionally difficult fast-switch regime, and report unrecovered-event rate.

### Citation

Luketina et al. (2022), Section 6.4 and appendix frequency experiments.

## E5 — Contextual adaptation depends on repeatable or generalizable context structure

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 3–4; conclusion
- **Claim:** Contextual meta-gradients are useful only to the extent that the chosen features let the learned meta-function generalize to recurring or related learning conditions.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The approach assumes that low-dimensional context features contain information relevant to good hyperparameter settings and that the learned function can generalize across those contexts. The paper emphasizes repeated structure as a favorable case while also testing a large set of Switching MDPs where exact tasks rarely recur and context generalization is harder.

### Thesis use

Do not treat a context signal as inherently informative. Validate it on held-out regimes and compare it against a fixed-hyperparameter baseline.

### Citation

Luketina et al. (2022), Sections 3–4 and conclusion.
