# Evidence — Robust-Gymnasium: A Unified Modular Benchmark for Robust Reinforcement Learning

## Evidence E1 — Disruptions can occur at different points in the agent–environment loop
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 2.1; Figure 2
- **Claim:** The framework distinguishes disruption of observed state/reward, disruption of the selected action before execution, and disruption of the environment dynamics or reward model itself.
- **Thesis use:** perturbation taxonomy; telemetry design
- **Topics:** observation disruption; action disruption; environment disruption
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Robust-Gymnasium models three distinct intervention points. An observation disruptor changes what state or reward signal reaches the agent; an action disruptor changes the action that is actually executed; and an environment disruptor changes the transition dynamics or reward function relative to the nominal environment. These mechanisms affect different parts of the causal interaction loop and should be logged separately.

### Thesis-safe implication
A GridWorld experiment should distinguish true state from observed state, intended action from executed action, and the active environment version or transition/reward regime.

## Evidence E2 — Target, mode, severity, and temporal frequency are separate perturbation dimensions
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 3.2–3.3; supplementary frequency experiments
- **Claim:** Robust-Gymnasium composes disruption targets with different disturbance modes and frequencies rather than representing robustness with one generic noise parameter.
- **Thesis use:** perturbation configuration
- **Topics:** mode; severity; frequency; onset
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
A disruption can target a state, reward, action, or the environment; be random, adversarial, or caused by an environmental shift; and occur at different temporal frequencies such as per step or per episode. Robustness conclusions therefore depend on both the type and schedule of the perturbation.

### Limitation
The thesis does not need to copy the benchmark's full factorial design. A small predeclared set of severity and frequency levels is sufficient if computational resources are limited.

## Evidence E3 — Nominal return does not establish robustness to post-training disruption
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 4.1–4.4
- **Claim:** Standard and robust RL methods can change relative performance substantially across different disruption targets and test-time conditions.
- **Thesis use:** clean-vs-shift evaluation
- **Topics:** nominal performance; post-training shift; robustness
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Performance in an undisturbed training environment is not enough to characterize behavior once sensing, actions, rewards, or environment dynamics are disrupted. The benchmark results show that methods can be strong under one disruption family and weak under another, making a single nominal score insufficient for robust-RL comparison.

### Thesis-safe implication
Report nominal return, immediate post-change degradation, worst-window performance, recovery trajectory when online learning is enabled, and final post-change performance separately.

## Evidence E4 — Robustness ranking is perturbation-specific
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4 and supplementary experiments
- **Claim:** No evaluated method is uniformly dominant across all disruption modes and stages.
- **Thesis use:** claim discipline
- **Topics:** benchmark ranking; perturbation family; robustness profile
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The benchmark exposes different strengths and weaknesses depending on which component of the interaction process is perturbed. An algorithm should therefore be described by a robustness profile over declared shift families rather than labeled globally “more robust” from one test condition.

## Evidence E5 — Robust-Gymnasium is primarily a robustness benchmark, not a complete online-recovery protocol
- **Type:** thesis-safe inference from the benchmark design
- **Location:** Sections 2–4; robust non-stationary extension discussion
- **Claim:** The benchmark supports disrupted and non-stationary tasks but does not define one universal detection-delay or time-to-recovery metric for continued post-shift learning.
- **Thesis use:** robustness/resilience boundary
- **Topics:** robustness benchmark; recovery; detector
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Robust-Gymnasium is valuable for systematically constructing disrupted MDPs and evaluating performance under them. A resilience study that permits continued learning after a hidden changepoint still needs an additional protocol for detection quality, transient degradation, and recovery timing.

## Avoid overclaiming
Use Robust-Gymnasium to justify perturbation taxonomy and modular benchmark design. Do not treat performance under a fixed disruption as equivalent to online resilience after an unannounced change.
