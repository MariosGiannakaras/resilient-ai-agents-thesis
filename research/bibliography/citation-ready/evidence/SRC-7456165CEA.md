# Evidence — Restarted Bayesian Online Change-point Detection for Non-Stationary Markov Decision Processes

## E1 — Abrupt non-stationarity can be modeled as piecewise-stationary MDP regimes

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Section 1; problem formulation
- **Claim:** The paper studies a sequence of stationary MDP regimes separated by unknown changepoints at which transition and reward distributions may change.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Alami, Mahfoud, and Moulines model the environment as a piecewise-stationary discrete-time MDP. Within each interval the MDP is stationary, while at unknown changepoints its transition distributions and rewards may change. The finite state and action sets remain fixed across regimes.

### Context and limits

The formulation targets abrupt switching and does not represent unrestricted continuous drift or changes to the state/action interface.

### Thesis use

Use this as a formal model for sudden persistent changes and log the true `change_step` for evaluation while keeping it hidden from non-oracle agents.

### Citation

Alami, Mahfoud, and Moulines (2023), Abstract, Section 1, and problem formulation.

## E2 — Change detection requires false-alarm and detection-delay metrics

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4.1, theoretical detector analysis
- **Claim:** The detector is analyzed through both premature alarms and the time required to identify a true change after it occurs.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The R-BOCPD analysis controls the probability of declaring a changepoint before the true change and derives detection-delay behavior after a change. These are distinct detector properties: a detector can react quickly by becoming overly sensitive, so delay cannot be interpreted without false-alarm behavior.

### Context and limits

The theoretical guarantees rely on the paper's multinomial observation model and threshold/confidence construction.

### Thesis use

Report false alarms, missed changes, and detection delay independently from return and recovery.

### Citation

Alami et al. (2023), Section 4.1.

## E3 — Detectability depends on statistical separation between regimes

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Theorem 4.3; Corollary 4.13
- **Claim:** Detection becomes easier as the pre- and post-change distributions become more statistically distinguishable.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The paper's delay and regret terms depend inversely on the KL divergence between transition distributions on adjacent stationary segments. Under its assumptions, larger distributional separation therefore supports faster reliable detection.

### Context and limits

KL divergence in the multinomial transition model is not automatically an interpretable severity measure for every possible GridWorld perturbation.

### Thesis use

Calibrate perturbation levels empirically so that severity and detectability are both characterized, rather than assuming geometric change magnitude equals statistical detectability.

### Citation

Alami et al. (2023), Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.13.

## E4 — Detection becomes adaptation only when connected to a response mechanism

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 4.2–4.3
- **Claim:** R-BOCPD-UCRL2 uses detected changes to restart the stationary learner and discard stale data.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The proposed RL system monitors transition observations with the changepoint detector. When a change is detected, the UCRL2 learning state is restarted so estimates from the previous regime no longer bias learning in the new regime.

### Context and limits

Restarting discards useful knowledge as well as stale knowledge and provides no context recall when a previous regime returns. Detection also does not imply immediate task recovery.

### Thesis use

Use detector-triggered full reset as a clear baseline distinct from continual updating and context-memory methods.

### Citation

Alami et al. (2023), Sections 4.2–4.3.

## E5 — Oracle and non-adaptive comparators clarify the source of gains

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 5 and Figure 2
- **Claim:** The experiments compare the detector-triggered method with vanilla learning, periodic restarts, sliding-window approaches, and a changepoint-aware oracle.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The evaluation includes alternatives that either ignore non-stationarity, forget data on a fixed schedule, use recency windows, or receive true changepoint information. This comparator structure separates the value of detection from the value of simply forgetting old observations.

### Thesis use

Retain no-detection, periodic/reset, recency, detector-triggered, and oracle-change baselines where feasible.

### Citation

Alami et al. (2023), Section 5 and Figure 2.

## E6 — The detector has explicit distributional and model-scope limitations

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Limitations and future work
- **Claim:** The implementation is tied to multinomial transition streams and is better suited to sufficiently large changes; model-free and more general local/global change handling remain open extensions.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The authors identify limitations including the multinomial observation assumption, difficulty with small changes and thresholding, and the absence of a model-free extension. Their theoretical construction should therefore not be described as a universal detector for every form of RL non-stationarity.

### Thesis use

Run a feasibility pilot and include missed-change analysis before adopting R-BOCPD as a final detector baseline.

### Citation

Alami et al. (2023), limitations and future work.
