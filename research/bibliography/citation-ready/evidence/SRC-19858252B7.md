# Evidence — Reinforcement Learning by Guided Safe Exploration

## E1 — Safe transfer should measure safety and task progress separately

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4.2; Figure 2
- **Claim:** The paper distinguishes safety jump-start and time to safety from return jump-start and time to optimal task performance.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Yang et al. define transfer metrics that separate how safely an agent begins the target task from how quickly it becomes safe, and separately measure the initial task-performance advantage and time required to reach optimal reward performance. The framework therefore makes explicit that safe behavior and rapid task learning can improve at different rates.

### Context and limits

The transfer metrics compare a learner that receives source knowledge with a learner trained from scratch. A gain in safety does not imply a gain in task-recovery speed, and vice versa.

### Thesis use

For context recall or pretrained safety mechanisms, report initial safety, time to safety, return jump-start, and time to task recovery separately.

### Citation

Yang et al. (2023), Section 4.2 and Figure 2.

## E2 — The safe guide is a prior capability learned in a controlled source environment

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Sections 1 and 4
- **Claim:** SaGui obtains safety knowledge through prior reward-free constrained training in a controlled source task and transfers that policy to the target learner.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The framework first trains a task-agnostic guide using safety costs in a controlled environment where unsafe interactions are permitted. After the target reward is revealed, the guide is transferred to the target task, used to generate safer initial behavior, and distilled into a student whose reliance on the guide is gradually reduced.

### Context and limits

Improved initial safety is therefore not free: it depends on source-task interactions, a mapping between source and target state spaces, shared actions, and an assumption that relevant safety dynamics transfer.

### Thesis use

Include source/pretraining interactions and prior model/task assumptions in the resource and fairness ledger.

### Citation

Yang et al. (2023), Abstract and Sections 1 and 4.

## E3 — Transfer requires explicit source–target compatibility assumptions

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4.1, Assumption 1 and source–target mapping
- **Claim:** The guide can be reused because source and target tasks share an action space and are connected through a state mapping, while the safety-relevant dynamics are assumed to be sufficiently compatible.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The target policy induced by the guide applies a mapping from target states to source states and reuses the source action distribution. The construction assumes the same action space and relies on the source environment preserving the safety information needed in the target.

### Context and limits

A structural shift that invalidates this mapping or changes safety dynamics can turn prior knowledge into negative transfer.

### Thesis use

Include a source–target mismatch ablation and a no-transfer baseline for any pretrained guide or context-recall mechanism.

### Citation

Yang et al. (2023), Section 4.1 and Assumption 1.

## E4 — Guidance and student learning are distinct components

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 1 and 4; Figure 1
- **Claim:** SaGui composes behavior from a pretrained safety guide and a target-task student, while policy distillation transfers guide behavior into the student over time.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The guide initially contributes strongly to behavior so the target agent can collect safer trajectories. The student simultaneously learns the revealed target objective and is regularized toward guide behavior while it remains unreliable. As training progresses, the framework reduces the guide's influence.

### Context and limits

The guide is not an environmental change detector, and the student's policy improvement should not be credited entirely to the guide.

### Thesis use

Log the fraction of actions attributable to guidance, guide interventions, and student-only performance separately.

### Citation

Yang et al. (2023), Sections 1 and 4 and Figure 1.
