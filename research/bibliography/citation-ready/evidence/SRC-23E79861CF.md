# Evidence — Multi-model Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes

## E1 — Model ambiguity can be represented as a belief over multiple plausible models
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Introduction; Section 3
- **Claim:** MPOMDPs represent several credible POMDP models that share the same structural spaces but differ in model parameters, and maintain a belief over which model best explains the system.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Li and Denton define model ambiguity as the presence of multiple credible optimization models with the same general structure but different transition, observation, reward, or related parameters. The MPOMDP retains these candidates jointly and associates each with a model-belief weight that represents its current plausibility or importance.

### Thesis use
Use this as formal background for a finite context/model library in which the agent maintains uncertainty over the active regime rather than committing immediately to one label.

### Citation
Li and Denton, Abstract, Introduction, and Section 3.

## E2 — Model beliefs are updated from observed system outputs
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Introduction; MPOMDP formulation
- **Claim:** The model-belief vector changes over time as observations provide evidence that is more or less likely under each candidate model.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The MPOMDP learns the distribution over candidate models from system outputs over time. As observations arrive, the belief assigned to each model is updated according to how well that model explains the observed sequence, allowing the policy to adapt its decisions as evidence accumulates.

### Context and limits
This update is inference over a predefined model set. It does not create a new model when the true regime lies outside the library.

### Thesis use
Compare hard context selection with belief-weighted control under the same observation history.

### Citation
Li and Denton, Introduction and model formulation.

## E3 — Belief-weighted optimization is different from worst-case robust optimization
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Introduction; Section 2
- **Claim:** MPOMDP optimizes a model-belief-weighted value objective and is therefore generally less conservative than max–min robust optimization.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper contrasts its objective with robust formulations that choose a policy for the worst plausible model. MPOMDP instead seeks one policy that performs well across candidate models according to their current belief weights. This weighted objective can exploit evidence that some models are more plausible rather than treating every model as an equally binding worst case.

### Thesis use
Treat `belief_weighted`, `hard_selection`, and `worst_case_fallback` as distinct response mechanisms in any context-library experiment.

### Citation
Li and Denton, Introduction and Section 2.

## E4 — If the true model is absent, the belief can prefer the closest candidate without eliminating misspecification
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Introduction; structural-results discussion
- **Claim:** Even when none of the candidate POMDPs is exactly the true model, the belief update can assign greater weight to a candidate with higher likelihood for the observed outputs.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The authors state that the model-belief mechanism continues to discriminate among the available candidates even when the true data-generating model is not included. Under the stated assumptions, candidates that are more capable of generating the observed outputs receive greater weight. This identifies a best-fitting available model but does not make the model library correctly specified.

### Context and limits
A confident belief over an incomplete library can still be confidently wrong about unmodeled structure.

### Thesis use
Include a test in which the active regime is deliberately absent from the context library and measure whether confidence remains appropriately cautious.

### Citation
Li and Denton, Introduction and structural-properties discussion.

## E5 — MPOMDP can be reformulated as a larger POMDP at additional computational cost
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Introduction; structural properties; Sections 4–5
- **Claim:** An MPOMDP can be represented as a special POMDP with an enlarged state space, inheriting POMDP structure while increasing computational burden.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The paper shows that the multi-model problem can be recast as a special-case POMDP by augmenting the state representation. This gives access to standard POMDP properties and solution ideas, but the authors emphasize the associated curse of dimensionality and history and develop approximation methods for practical solution.

### Thesis use
Report memory and compute growth as the number of stored regimes increases; do not treat context-library size as free.

### Citation
Li and Denton, Introduction and Sections 4–5.

## E6 — MPOMDP is a formal model-ambiguity framework, not a direct resilience algorithm
- **Type:** thesis-scope synthesis
- **Location:** Overall formulation
- **Claim:** The paper addresses sequential decision making under ambiguity among candidate models; it does not define changepoint detection, reset policies, or repeated-shift recovery metrics.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
Use MPOMDP to justify belief-aware context handling, while evaluating change detection and temporal recovery with separate mechanisms and metrics.

### Citation
Li and Denton, overall formulation.
