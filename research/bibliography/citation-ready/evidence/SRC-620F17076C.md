# Evidence — Robust Deep Reinforcement Learning against Adversarial Perturbations on State Observations

## E1 — Observation corruption changes the policy input, not the true environment state

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 1; Figure 1; Section 3.1
- **Claim:** In the state-adversarial MDP, the agent acts from a perturbed observation while the environment transition still originates from the true state.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

Zhang et al. model a state-observation adversary that replaces the agent's observation of true state `s` with an allowed perturbed observation `nu(s)`. The policy chooses its action from `nu(s)`, but the transition kernel evolves the environment from the underlying true state `s` using the selected action.

### Context and limits

This is an observation-uncertainty model. It should not be conflated with transition-dynamics changes or action-execution failures.

### Thesis use

Log true state and observed state separately in observation-corruption scenarios.

### Citation

Zhang et al. (2020), Section 1, Figure 1, and Section 3.1.

## E2 — Worst-case observation perturbation is different from a fixed stochastic observation model

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 1; Section 3.1
- **Claim:** The SA-MDP represents adversarial worst-case observation choice within an allowed set rather than a fixed conditional observation distribution.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The paper motivates SA-MDP because a standard POMDP observation kernel does not encode an adversary that deliberately selects an allowed observation to reduce return. The adversarial formulation therefore addresses a different uncertainty model from ordinary non-adversarial sensor noise.

### Context and limits

For stochastic measurement noise without an attacker, a probabilistic observation model or POMDP may be more appropriate.

### Thesis use

Label adversarial observation attacks separately from stochastic observation corruption.

### Citation

Zhang et al. (2020), Section 1 and Section 3.1.

## E3 — The robust control problem does not inherit all ordinary-MDP optimality properties

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 3.1, Theorems 1–4
- **Claim:** Bellman evaluation is available for fixed policy/adversary combinations, but an optimal stationary Markov policy need not exist under the optimal observation adversary.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

For fixed policy and adversary, the induced system can be evaluated with Bellman equations. When optimizing against the worst observation adversary, however, the paper shows that the standard existence properties of ordinary MDPs do not transfer automatically. This motivates a constrained policy-improvement approach rather than naively reusing supervised adversarial-training ideas.

### Thesis use

State the exact robustness assumptions of any observation-robust baseline rather than treating it as an ordinary MDP with noise added informally.

### Citation

Zhang et al. (2020), Section 3.1, Theorems 1–4.

## E4 — Local policy smoothness provides a principled robustness surrogate

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 3.2; Theorem 5
- **Claim:** The paper regularizes policies so action distributions change little within an allowed neighborhood of perturbed observations.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The proposed robustness objective controls the difference between the policy at the true state and policies evaluated at nearby admissible observations. The paper connects this requirement to total-variation and KL-style distances and derives algorithm-specific practical regularizers.

### Context and limits

The perturbation neighborhood must have semantic meaning for the chosen state representation.

### Thesis use

A simple tabular analogue can measure action disagreement or policy variation under bounded observation corruption.

### Citation

Zhang et al. (2020), Section 3.2 and Theorem 5.

## E5 — Clean and attacked performance should be reported together

- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 4; Tables 1–3
- **Claim:** The empirical evaluation reports natural performance separately from performance under observation attacks.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The experiments compare agents under unperturbed execution and under several white-box observation attacks, and in some cases also report action-certification behavior. This exposes whether robustness gains under attack are accompanied by changes in nominal performance.

### Context and limits

Adversarial attack results do not directly imply robustness to ordinary stochastic noise, and the paper's algorithm ranking should not be transferred to a different benchmark.

### Thesis use

Report nominal return, perturbed return, and policy/action disagreement separately.

### Citation

Zhang et al. (2020), Section 4 and Tables 1–3.

## E6 — This is pre-emptive robustness, not online recovery

- **Type:** scope inference grounded in the paper
- **Location:** Sections 1–4
- **Claim:** The robust policies are trained before test-time attacks; the paper does not study unknown changepoints, detection, or continued post-change learning.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase

The contribution improves policies so they are less sensitive to bounded observation perturbations at evaluation time. It does not define a detector or an adaptation trajectory after an environmental regime change.

### Thesis use

Keep observation robustness separate from resilience metrics such as detection delay and recovery time.

### Citation

Zhang et al. (2020), overall method scope, Sections 1–4.
