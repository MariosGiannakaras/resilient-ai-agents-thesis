# Evidence — Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning Methods for Nonstationary Environments

## Evidence E1 — Safe adaptation can require both performance and safety mechanisms to remain valid after change
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Section 1
- **Claim:** Non-stationarity can degrade task performance and also undermine safety assumptions or constraints that were designed for a stationary environment.
- **Thesis use:** safety/performance separation
- **Topics:** safe adaptation; changing constraints; non-stationarity
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
A safe RL method deployed in a changing environment may need to adapt not only the policy or learning mechanism but also the way safety constraints are interpreted or parameterized. An initially adequate safety rule can become ineffective after the environment changes, so performance recovery alone is insufficient evidence of safe continual adaptation.

### Limitation
This does not mean every thesis experiment needs adaptive constraints. If safety rules are fixed by design, the experiment should state that explicitly and measure whether they remain satisfied after the shift.

## Evidence E2 — Non-stationarity should be identified by the MDP/CMDP component that changes and by its origin
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 2.3–2.4
- **Claim:** The survey distinguishes exogenous, endogenous, and hybrid non-stationarity and discusses changes to reward, transition dynamics, action distributions/spaces, and constraints.
- **Thesis use:** shift taxonomy
- **Topics:** passive non-stationarity; active non-stationarity; reward shift; dynamics shift
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The term non-stationarity covers several different mechanisms. A change can be caused by external factors, by the learning agent's own behavior, or by both, and it can affect different parts of the decision process. Experiments should therefore state which component changes instead of using a generic “dynamic environment” label.

## Evidence E3 — Recovery speed and transient safety are separate requirements
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 3, problem formulation and adaptation discussion
- **Claim:** Continual safe RL must consider how quickly performance recovers and whether safety constraints remain satisfied during the adaptation interval.
- **Thesis use:** recovery and safety metrics
- **Topics:** adaptation time; transient safety; constraint violations
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Fast return to acceptable task performance is only one dimension of safe adaptation. The agent can recover reward quickly while violating constraints during the transient period, so a safe evaluation should pair recovery-time measures with violation counts, cumulative safety cost, or other declared constraint metrics over the same interval.

## Evidence E4 — Adaptation approaches differ by when and how they respond to non-stationarity
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** method taxonomy in Sections 5–6
- **Claim:** The surveyed methods include passive, reactive, rapid-adaptation, and proactive responses to changing environments.
- **Thesis use:** related-work organization
- **Topics:** reactive adaptation; proactive adaptation; safety layer
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Some approaches remain inside known safe regions without explicitly reacting to a detected change; others update after a change is observed, use meta/context mechanisms for rapid adaptation, or try to anticipate future conditions. These categories encode different information assumptions and should not be ranked as if they solved the same problem under the same oracle access.

## Evidence E5 — Safety-constraint formulations differ in what is allowed to change
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 7 and constraint taxonomy
- **Claim:** Constraints can be fixed, partially adjustable, or adjustable with respect to time or data.
- **Thesis use:** safety formulation disclosure
- **Topics:** hard constraint; soft constraint; threshold; context-dependent safety
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
A constraint may keep both its cost function and threshold fixed, change only part of that specification, or adapt both as the environment evolves. These choices imply different guarantees and evaluation criteria. Any safe baseline in the thesis should declare its constraint function, threshold, and whether either is allowed to change.

## Evidence E6 — Many safe non-stationary methods rely on smooth-change assumptions or provide soft guarantees
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 8, limitations and open problems
- **Claim:** Existing methods often rely on bounded/smooth non-stationarity assumptions and frequently provide probabilistic, expected, or budget-style safety guarantees rather than uninterrupted hard guarantees.
- **Thesis use:** guarantee labeling
- **Topics:** smoothness; probabilistic guarantee; violation budget
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Safety claims in non-stationary RL depend strongly on assumptions about how quickly the environment can change and what prior safety information is available. A guarantee expressed as an expected cost bound, probability bound, or variation budget should not be described as an unconditional hard safety guarantee.

## Avoid overclaiming
This source is a survey and should be used to classify safety/adaptation assumptions and reporting requirements. It does not establish a universal safe continual-RL algorithm or require adaptive constraints in the thesis core.
