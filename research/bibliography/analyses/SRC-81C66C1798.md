## Material type and scope

This paper is a broad literature review of how AI is used to enhance resilience across many application domains: infrastructure, disaster response, climate, healthcare, business continuity, cybersecurity, communities, supply chains, cities, ecosystems, hospitality/tourism, and psychological well-being.

Its subject is primarily **AI as a tool for making other systems or processes more resilient**. That is related to, but importantly different from, the thesis's central problem: measuring and improving the resilience/adaptation/recovery behavior of AI agents themselves under controlled environmental change and uncertainty.

The paper is therefore useful for conceptual synthesis and terminology, but it should not determine the formal definition of resilient AI agents, the model set, the GridWorld protocol, or algorithm-level conclusions.

## Methodology

Section 2 describes a literature-review process using Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, PubMed, and ScienceDirect with keywords spanning AI and multiple forms of resilience. The selected literature is categorized by application domain and compared in terms of methodologies, AI technologies, outcomes, limitations, case studies, and real-world applications.

The methodology provides a clear narrative description of databases, keywords, categorization, and intended synthesis. However, the paper does not report the level of reproducibility expected from a rigorous systematic review: there is no complete search date/range, database-specific query syntax, result count, deduplication count, inclusion/exclusion flow, formal study-quality assessment, or PRISMA-style accounting. This weakens its use as citation-grade evidence for comparative claims about AI effectiveness.

## Thesis-content value

The paper remains useful for the thesis because it provides:

- a broad view of resilience as the ability to withstand, adapt to, and recover from adversity;
- examples of predictive, monitoring, decision-support, anomaly-detection, and recovery-oriented AI mechanisms across domains;
- terminology around robustness, continuity, adaptation, recovery, vulnerability, and proactive response;
- examples showing that resilience is multidimensional and domain-dependent;
- a broad catalogue of application areas that can help distinguish **resilient AI** from **AI for resilience**;
- discussion material for explaining why the thesis narrows its experimental scope to agent behavior under controlled change rather than attempting to cover every domain where AI may support resilience.

This content should remain fully searchable in the complete research corpus even though the source is not promoted into the strict citation package.

## Citation-grade limitations

1. **Scope mismatch:** the central object of resilience is usually infrastructure, organizations, communities, healthcare systems, supply chains, or people, not an RL agent adapting after a distribution/dynamics shift.
2. **Very broad synthesis:** the paper spans heterogeneous scientific fields whose definitions, datasets, outcomes, and validation standards are not directly comparable.
3. **Limited systematic-review reporting:** the search and screening description is insufficient for reproducing the exact literature set or estimating selection bias.
4. **No common benchmark:** claims about AI benefits synthesize results from unrelated domains rather than controlled head-to-head experiments.
5. **Bibliographic anomaly:** the DOI printed in the original PDF, `10.48185/jaai.v5i2.1053`, appears reused by the publisher across more than one file/article in the same issue. For this repository, title/authors were therefore verified from the primary PDF and official article page rather than trusting DOI-only metadata. This does not invalidate the PDF content, but it reduces confidence in automated bibliographic resolution.
6. **Redundancy with stronger sources:** the thesis corpus already contains more direct sources for non-stationary RL, robust MDPs, recovery measurement, GridWorld evaluation, uncertainty, continual adaptation, and resilient-agent definitions.

## Safe use in the thesis project

Use this material for:

- conceptual discovery;
- terminology checks;
- explaining the difference between AI-enabled resilience of an external system and resilience of the AI agent itself;
- broad drafting context;
- identifying primary papers or domain examples for later review.

Do not use it by itself for:

- selecting an RL algorithm;
- defining a formal resilience metric;
- claiming empirical superiority of AI over non-AI methods;
- setting GridWorld perturbation severity, recovery thresholds, or statistical protocol;
- claiming that results in infrastructure/healthcare/cybersecurity transfer to a single-agent MDP.
