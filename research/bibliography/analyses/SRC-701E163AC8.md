## Non-stationarity and recency
The second-edition material explicitly treats non-stationary bandit problems. Sample-average estimates are appropriate when reward distributions are stationary, but can adapt poorly when action values change over time. A constant step-size update gives more weight to recent rewards and exponentially less weight to older observations.

This is directly useful for the thesis baseline rationale: a constant/recency-sensitive update is a simple **continual adaptation mechanism**, not a changepoint detector. It should therefore be evaluated separately from detector-triggered resets or context recall.

## Q-learning baseline
Q-learning is presented as off-policy TD control, with the learned action-value function directly approximating the optimal action-value function while the behavior policy determines which state–action pairs are visited and updated.

For the thesis, this supports tabular Q-learning as the stationary/continual reference learner. It does not imply resilience by itself: after an environmental change, the stored value estimates may be stale and recovery depends on continued exploration, learning rate, visitation, and the nature of the shift.

## Critical distinctions for the experiment
- **Constant step size ≠ change detector.** It continuously discounts older evidence but does not declare when a regime changed.
- **Q-learning ≠ resilience guarantee.** It is a learning rule; post-shift recovery must be measured empirically.
- **Stationary convergence theory ≠ non-stationary tracking guarantee.** Classical convergence conditions should not be used to claim optimal tracking under repeated changes.
- **Recency ≠ reset.** A constant step size retains prior estimates with decaying influence; a reset discards them explicitly.
