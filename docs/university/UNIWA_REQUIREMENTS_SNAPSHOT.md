## Required organization

The reviewed guidance organizes the thesis into:
- front matter,
- main text,
- bibliography/references/web sources,
- appendices where needed.

Front matter includes title pages in both languages, copyright/plagiarism declarations, Greek summary/keywords, English abstract/keywords, table of contents, lists of figures/tables and terminology index.

## Indicative length

- No strict page limit is stated.
- Typical indicative size: approximately 70–140 pages or 20,000–40,000 words, excluding bibliography and appendices.
- Greek and English summaries: approximately 250–300 words.
- Usually around five keywords in each language.

## Content expectations

- The introduction identifies the problem, purpose, research questions, methodology, contribution/innovation and document structure.
- Literature/background sections should be comparative and critical.
- Methodology records decisions, parameters, conditions, sources of data, equipment and software to permit repetition/verification.
- The technical part presents analysis, design, implementation and results.
- Results are organized by experiments/conditions and compared with known work.
- Conclusions answer the initial questions and state limitations, difficulties and future directions.

## Formatting snapshot

- A4.
- Times New Roman 11 pt.
- 1.5 line spacing.
- 14 pt headings, 12 pt subheadings, preferably bold.
- Structured numbered heading styles and automatic table of contents.
- New chapter starts on a new page.
- Figures/tables are numbered, captioned and sourced where required.
- Table text should not be smaller than 10 pt according to the guidance.
- Equations should use an equation editor and numbering where appropriate.
- Greek/English technical terms and acronyms are introduced consistently.
- One international referencing style is used consistently; the source lists APA 6, Chicago 16, IEEE 2006 and Harvard Anglia 2008.
