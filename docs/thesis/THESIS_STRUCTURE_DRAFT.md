# Draft Thesis Structure

**Status:** Provisional. It must be validated against the current official template, the final research question and supervisor direction. Chapter boundaries may be merged to avoid fragmentation.

## Front matter
- Greek title page.
- English title page.
- Copyright/plagiarism declarations.
- Optional dedication/acknowledgements.
- Greek summary and keywords.
- English abstract and keywords.
- Table of contents.
- Lists of figures/tables.
- Glossary/acronyms/terms.

## 1. Introduction
- Problem context and relevance.
- Official purpose and scope.
- Research questions and, if appropriate, hypotheses.
- Expected contribution.
- Method overview.
- Thesis structure.

## 2. Theoretical background
- Intelligent agents and decision-making.
- Decision-making, learning, planning or other concepts required by the methods ultimately selected.
- Uncertainty, non-stationarity, partial observability and resilience definitions.
- Evaluation and reproducibility concepts.

## 3. Related work
- Resilient/adaptive agents.
- GridWorld and controlled benchmark approaches.
- Recovery/robustness metrics.
- Relevant comparative studies.
- Identified gap and thesis positioning.

## 4. GridWorld environment and uncertainty model
- GridWorld landscape review, build/reuse/adapt decision and source/license provenance.
- Nominal state/action/transition/reward specification.
- Disturbance taxonomy and severity.
- Scenario/configuration generation.
- Validation and known-answer examples.

## 5. Compared models and algorithms
- Baselines.
- Selected agent/method families.
- Information/adaptation assumptions.
- Implementation sources and versions.
- Inclusion/exclusion rationale.

## 6. Methodology and experimental design
- Research design and variables.
- Training/tuning/evaluation split.
- Experiment matrix.
- Seeds, repetitions, budgets and stopping.
- Metrics and statistical analysis plan.
- Failure/exclusion policy.
- Threat controls and reproducibility.

## 7. Software architecture and implementation
- Core/CLI architecture.
- Experiment runner and lifecycle.
- Persistence, schemas and provenance.
- Tests and validation.
- Hardware/software environment.
- Significant decisions and limitations.

## 8. Dashboard and visualization
- Purpose and requirements.
- Information architecture.
- Run configuration/control.
- Live GridWorld and truthful progress/logging.
- Comparison, result exploration and export.
- Screenshots and usability limitations.

## 9. Experimental results
- Dataset/run-set completeness.
- Nominal baseline performance.
- Effects by uncertainty type/severity.
- Degradation and recovery.
- Model comparisons with intervals/effect sizes.
- Generalization and resource results.
- Failures, exclusions and sensitivity analyses.

## 10. Discussion
- Direct answers to research questions.
- Interpretation relative to theory and related work.
- Trade-offs and boundary conditions.
- Negative/unexpected results.
- Practical and scientific implications.

## 11. Threats to validity and limitations
- Internal, construct, external, statistical and reproducibility threats.
- Scope/hardware/environment limitations.
- Mitigations and residual uncertainty.

## 12. Conclusions and future work
- Objective and method recap.
- Evidence-supported findings.
- Contributions.
- Unresolved issues.
- Realistic extensions.

## Bibliography

Only verified sources cited in the text.

## Appendices
- Full protocol/config schemas.
- Extended tables/plots.
- Additional validation traces.
- Reproduction instructions.
- Relevant code/config excerpts, not an unnecessary full source dump.
- Run/artifact provenance register.
