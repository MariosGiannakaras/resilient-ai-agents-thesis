## 3. Current mandatory / controlling structure

The Department writing guide orders the thesis into three major groups.

### 3.1 Initial matter

1. Title page in the thesis language, matching the cover.
2. Title page in the other language.
3. Copyright and plagiarism declarations.
4. Dedication, optional.
5. Acknowledgements, optional.
6. Greek summary and keywords.
7. English abstract and keywords.
8. Table of contents with page numbers.
9. List of figures.
10. List of tables.
11. Alphabetical index/glossary of terms.

The current project will also maintain a controlled abbreviation/acronym list because the thesis uses a large technical vocabulary. It supplements, rather than replaces, the required term/index material.

### 3.2 Main text

- Introduction.
- Numbered substantive chapters.
- Final conclusions chapter.

The exact scientific chapter decomposition is not prescribed by the Department guide; it is selected in T-701 to fit this research design while preserving the required Introduction-to-Conclusions structure.

### 3.3 Final matter

- Bibliography / references / web sources, not numbered as a normal chapter.
- Appendices A/B/C as needed.

## 4. Current formatting contract

The Department guide continues to support the following working Word contract:

- A4 page size.
- Times New Roman, 11 pt main text.
- 1.5 line spacing.
- Main chapter headings: 14 pt, preferably bold.
- Subheadings: 12 pt, preferably bold.
- Numbered heading hierarchy suitable for automatic Table of Contents generation.
- Each new chapter starts on a new page.
- Paragraph spacing is controlled through paragraph properties rather than manually inserted blank lines.
- Figures and tables are numbered and captioned; non-original material receives source attribution.
- Equations use proper equation tooling and numbering where appropriate.
- Tables remain readable; the historical/current Department snapshot specifies not reducing table text below approximately 10 pt.
- Technical terminology is introduced consistently; Greek and English forms are paired on first use where useful for unambiguous technical meaning.
- Correct grammar and academic register are required; colloquial or unexplained technical slang is avoided.
- Detailed source code, very large tables, long diagnostic listings, exhaustive numerical material and supplementary technical detail belong in appendices when they would interrupt the main argument.

The final review-ready `.docx` will implement this with real Word Heading styles, caption fields, cross-references, automatic TOC, automatic lists of figures/tables, controlled section/page breaks and a final field update/visual inspection.

## 5. Scale

The Department guide provides an indicative, not mandatory, scale of approximately:

- 70–140 pages; and
- 20,000–40,000 words,

excluding bibliography and appendices.

Greek and English summaries are indicated at approximately 250–300 words, commonly with about five keywords each.

These are quality/scale references, not quotas. The thesis will not be padded to hit a page count.

## 6. Academic / institutional obligations

Article 28 of the current public undergraduate-programme regulation establishes that:

- the Diploma Thesis is individual work;
- it carries 30 ECTS in the five-year programme;
- it is an autonomous scientific and systematic treatment of a topic grounded in existing literature/research;
- plagiarism is prohibited and the work is subject to electronic plagiarism checking;
- the final text includes the required author declaration;
- examination is by a three-member committee according to Department/University governance.

The exact official author declaration used at final composition must be taken from the then-current Department/University wording, not reconstructed from an example thesis.

## 7. Citation-style decision for WP7 drafting

The Department guide requires one citation/reference style to be used consistently and historically lists multiple acceptable styles rather than imposing one unique style.

For this Computer Engineering / AI / experimental research thesis, **IEEE numeric citation style is selected as the WP7 drafting and final-reference default**, because:

1. it is one of the styles explicitly recognized by the Department guidance;
2. numeric bracket citation practice is dominant in the technically closest contextual ICE examples reviewed in T-701; and
3. it fits the engineering/AI literature and dense evidence mapping used by this project.

This is a project writing decision, **not a claim that ICE mandates IEEE**. A later explicit supervisor or Department instruction supersedes it and triggers a controlled citation-style conversion/audit.
