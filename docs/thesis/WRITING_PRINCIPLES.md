# Writing Principles

1. Use academic but understandable Greek.
2. Keep terminology consistent; define Greek and English technical terms on first use.
3. Separate:
   - established facts and cited literature,
   - design proposals,
   - frozen methodology,
   - observed results,
   - interpretation,
   - speculation and future work.
4. Do not overstate causality, generality or superiority.
5. State the unit of analysis, uncertainty and limitations around every quantitative conclusion.
6. Report negative, null and unexpected results.
7. Report failed methods/runs when relevant to validity.
8. Do not invent citations, DOI, quotes, page numbers, data or results.
9. Verify every bibliography entry against the original source.
10. Attribute non-original figures/tables and respect licenses.
11. Avoid using AI-generated prose as authority.
12. Avoid unsupported adjectives such as “optimal”, “robust”, “significant” or “state-of-the-art”.
13. Reserve “statistically significant” for a defined inferential analysis; prefer effect magnitude and intervals.
14. Connect major conclusions to exact figure/table/run IDs.
15. Do not transcribe numbers manually when a generated source can be referenced.
16. Explain methodological choices and important rejected alternatives.
17. Document protocol deviations and post-hoc analyses as such.
18. Keep the dashboard chapter subordinate to the research objective.
19. Do not present engineering convenience as scientific contribution without argument.
20. Use captions that state what is shown, conditions, aggregation and uncertainty.
21. Make cross-references and TOC/list fields automatic in Word.
22. Maintain a glossary/acronym register.
23. Use passive/impersonal academic constructions where natural, without making prose obscure.
24. Rewrite translated English concepts into correct Greek syntax rather than literal translation.
25. Final proofreading must check language, logic, citations, figures, cross-references and evidence traceability separately.
